Revisit our example:

We want to prove that for any positive integer n:

    1 + 2 + ... + n = n*(n+1)/2


HOW TO USE THIS STRUCTURE

The above claim is P(n)

Proof by induction.

Base case - We must prove the claim holds for the first positive integer n,
n = 1. We have that the left side of the claim is 1, and the right side of the claim is 1(1+1)/2 = 1. The two sides are equal, so the base case holds

    Inductive Step- We assume the inductive hypothesis - 
        that 1 + 2 + ... + k = k(k+1)/2 
    for some fixed positive integer k. We must show that our claim holds for k+1, that is we must show that: 
        1 + 2 + ... + k + (K+1) = (K+1)((K+1)+1)/2
    
    We have that:
    1 + 2 + ... + k + (K +1) = K(K+1)/2 + (k+1) (by inductive hypothesis)

Example: Prove that 3^n - 1 is even for all positive integers n.


what does "even" mean?  divisible by 2
    {x: x = 2k, k in z, K >=1 }

domain: natural numbers


Proof by induction.
Base case - we must prove that that our claim holds for the smallest positive integer, n = 1. We have that 3^1 - 1 = 2
 since 2 = 2*1 where 1 is an integer, we have that 2 is even. The base case holds

 Inductive Step: we assume the inductive hypothesis-- that 3^k - 1 is even for some fixed positive integer k. 
    we must prove that 3^(k+1) - 1 is also even
    Since 3^(k+1) - 1 is even, we have 3^k - 1 = 2a where a is an integer.
    We have that:
    3^(k+1) - 1. = 3*3^k - 1
                 = 3*(3^k - 1) - 1 + 3
                 = 3*(3^k - 1) + 2
                 = 3*2a + 2
                 = 6a + 2
                 = 2(3a + 1)

and so 3^(k+1) - 1 = 2(3a+1), where 3a + 1 is an integer thus 3^(k+1) - 1 is even
